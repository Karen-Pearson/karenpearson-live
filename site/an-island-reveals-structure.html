An Island Reveals Structure Better Than Any Book

The Island · July 27, 2026 · Karen Pearson

I live on Decatur Island, in the Salish Sea, in relative isolation. No bridge reaches here. To an expert in convenience, that is an inconvenience. To an old soul, it is the design.

An island reveals structure better than any book, because it makes visible the internal support system that convenience usually hides. On the mainland, the structure that sustains your life is obscured by how easy everything is to reach. Water arrives, power arrives, food arrives, help arrives. You can go years without seeing the system that holds you up, which means you can go years pretending it is not yours to hold.

Out here, every requirement is anticipated, transported, and maintained by me. The consequences of neglect are expressed immediately, and sometimes permanently. The generator does not care about my reasons. The water tank does not negotiate. The apples get picked before the rain or they do not get picked. The island does not teach any of this in a didactic sense. It does something cleaner. It removes the conditions that allow the structure to be ignored.

And here is the part that matters to you, wherever you live. Each person has a structure, an underlying system through which their life is sustained. Yours is running right now. The only question is whether the conditions of your life let you ignore it. Most lives are arranged, carefully and expensively, to keep that system out of view. We call the arrangement comfort. Then some weather arrives, some outage, some loss, and people discover they never learned where their own load-bearing walls are.

From here, I do one kind of work. I identify the structures people operate from but rarely see. The island is my instrument for the same reason a cleared table is a carpenter’s. Nothing on it but what is being examined.

Decatur’s location also dissolves a seduction the mainland runs constantly, the bargaining for existence that blurs merit with overinvestment. Out here, consciousness sets the agenda no one announced. Chores become truth playing in the rain. The trees rustle with a mystery that is somehow familiar. I did not move to the edge of the map to escape the world. I came here to see it clearly. This is not the edge of anything. It is the vantage point.

You do not have to move to an island. You have to notice what your conveniences are hiding. What would show up in the first week if the deliveries stopped? That answer is a map of your structure. Read it before the weather does.

Live deliberately.
