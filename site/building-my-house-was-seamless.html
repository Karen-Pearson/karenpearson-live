Building My House Was Seamless

July 29, 2026 · Karen Pearson

I heard that you did not experience any problems with the county. What do you attribute this to?

When I was asked this yesterday, I quickly and answered, “my attitude.” That reply was met with a sour face. The questioner wanted a freebee, a quick fix, a secret bypass to the county mandates and policies that govern the building process. People know where they live. Unless you build outside the lines of governmental jurisdiction, you are subject to permitting. But the permitting is not the obstacle. Any problems that arise in any situation are always based on expectations.

My answer is as follows.
I understand humanity as doing its absolute best in any given moment.

The government in place today today evolved from archaic, thousand years old norms to promote the survival of the species. Currently, most of the world has access to the basics of food, water, and shelter, resulting in a clash between the governement’s archaic agenda and the evolving needs of humanity.

Those individuals employed by the government, receive the brunt of the conflict, as they only represent agency. They act with limited or no power to create, change, or bypass the policies. They are doing the absolute best they can within the limited scope of their environment. They want to do a good job.

I only ever addressed the county and its employees. I never dealt with them. Addressing people and situations means every one and every thing present is noble. Dealing with people and situations means every one and every thing present is a problem. I only ever address.

I understood that while I am submitting paperwork, the employees job’s were a series of navigating that paperwork through obstacles, heirarchies, and power plays. Much akin to driving a baby strapped to the backseat of a bicycle with no food or water and one flat tire through the Amazon jungle, and communication only available every seven hundred miles at the government-mandated telephone booths. So, my only job was to supply as all the information required on the forms to get the employee and the paperwork both to the end of their journey alive and well.

I found that any question from the county was always my oversight, and easily resolved on my end. I learned that every revision, clarification, or addition served to broaden my understanding of the codes and my perspective of the people working on my behalf. In every request from the county, the responsibility was always mine. And, the county always gave me the benefit of the doubt. It never stopped the process of permitting because I had ommitted information or forgot to attach a document. This kept all my power with me. So, I was ble to change, adapt, and evolve with the experience, which resulted in a smooth process.

Every person has good intentions. The only problem that arises with this statement is the misunderstanding that “every person” is someone other than me. There is no other. Given this understanding forms the foundation of my behavior and, inevitably, my attitude. I am delusional to think this understanding comes from anything other constant monitoring of my thoughts, challenging my beliefs, and reflecting on my behavior. What can I do to facilitate the smooth process of permitting? What can I do to make sure the county has all the information it needs to do its job seamlessly? There are no mysteries in the permit process.

We all do our best, but that looks like something different to each individual due of their own programming or history. Allowing each person their own story to play out as needed, curtails the need to set expectations for others to meet my needs, and rather shifts the focus appropriately to my own duty to self.

Live deliberately.
Karen
